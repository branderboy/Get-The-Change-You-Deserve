Why this campaign exists

Who Is This For

The bullseye is narrow on purpose: independent voters aged 18 to 34. Independent is overwhelmingly a young person’s registration. Nationally 47% of adults 18 to 29 identify as independents, more than any generation before them at the same age, and 57% of independents 18 to 34 have never been registered with either party at all. They are not lapsed Democrats or Republicans. They started outside and stayed there.

That is the group this campaign is built for. They carry no party telling them how to vote, they were shut out of the closed primary, they are the least likely to have voted before, and they are the cheapest people in the city to reach because they already live on the platforms this plan runs on. No other campaign in this race is aimed at them.

Around that center the same work reaches the rest of the city: older independents, working families, longtime Washingtonians, renters, small-business owners, and residents who follow D.C. news and culture on social media. They are the spillover, not the target.

Independent D.C. voters

77,286

17.2% of the city’s registered electorate.

This is not a small niche. It is a citywide audience larger than D.C.’s Republican registration base, and large enough to matter across wards, neighborhoods, and community conversations.

Why This Works

Four Numbers Behind The Bet

  • 80% of adults 18 to 29 use Instagram, 63% use TikTok. For 65 and over it is 19% and 12%. Mail and television make us pay to reach everyone else first.
  • 9% is average turnout for 18 to 34 in big-city mayoral races. They do not skip elections because they are hard to reach. They skip because nobody runs at them.
  • 28% is what youth turnout hit in the 2025 New York mayoral race. Single digits to 28% in one cycle, because a campaign was built for them. This is not theory.
  • 17.2% of registered D.C. voters carry no party, and they are concentrated. Ward 2 is 20.90%, Ward 3 is 19.05%. A small budget aimed at those blocks behaves like a much larger one.

Every other campaign is spending on people who already vote. Those voters are expensive, contested, and mostly decided. These are not.

Platform use: Pew Research Center, Americans’ Social Media Use, fielded February to June 2025. Mayoral youth turnout: CIRCLE at Tufts University. Registration shares: D.C. Board of Elections monthly report, as of August 31, 2026.
