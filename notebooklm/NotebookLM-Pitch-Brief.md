# Rhonda Hamilton for Mayor 2026 — Digital Social Media Campaign Pitch Brief

Source document for the audio briefing. Every number here comes from the campaign audit. Read the numbers as written.

## Opening line
D.C. has heard a lot of promises. This campaign is built on something different. Proof.

## The objective
Build broad visibility, repeated familiarity, and cultural relevance for Rhonda Hamilton across Washington, D.C. This is an organic-first awareness strategy. Make Hamilton known, recognizable, relevant, and credible before residents encounter advertising, local news, events, or direct outreach.

The resident outcome we are building toward: "I keep seeing Rhonda Hamilton. I know who she is. I understand that she is independent. She seems real. She talks about D.C. issues in a way I understand. I remember her name."

## What D.C. already told us (the audit)
We did not guess what people care about. We listened.

- 77 percent of residents call cost of living very important.
- 73 percent call public safety very important.
- 71 percent call housing very important.
  (City Cast DC and TrueDot poll, May 2026, 735 adults, margin of error plus or minus 3.7.)
- More than half of voters say D.C. is on the wrong track. More than half say D.C. is unaffordable for them. (Washington Post and Schar School, June 2026.)
- 79 percent oppose the federal takeover of D.C. police. 78 percent feel safe in their neighborhood. 54 percent believe crime is improving. 31 percent still call crime extremely or very serious. Concern about safety is not the same as support for federal control. (Washington Post and Schar School, August 2025, 604 residents.)
- Over 40 percent of voters east of the Anacostia River were undecided shortly before the primary. (Washington Post and Schar School, June 2026.)
- The 2026 primary turnout was 32 percent: about 155,940 ballots from 485,398 registered voters. Two thirds sat out. (DC Board of Elections.)
- About 91,000 D.C. voters, 18.4 percent of the electorate, are registered independent or unaffiliated. None of them could vote in the closed primary. (DC Board of Elections registration data.)
- Trash and parking enforcement were the highest-volume 311 categories in July 2026. (DC Open Data.)

The audience: the two thirds who sat out the primary, the 91,000 independents who could not vote in it, and the undecided east of the river. They are already asking questions. We answer them.

## The critics, answered
1. "Who is Rhonda Hamilton?" We answer with her story, in a recurring series called "Who Is Rhonda Hamilton?" Not a slogan.
2. "Is she a serious citywide candidate?" We show consistent activity, real resident conversations, issue fluency, community proof, events, and organization. Every week.
3. "What does independent mean?" A content pillar called "Independent, Explained." Independence as accountability to residents, not isolation.
4. "What has she actually done?" A pillar called "Proof, Not Slogans." ANC service, nonprofit leadership, mental-health advocacy, business experience.
5. "She lost in 2022, so why run again?" A response called "Why I Ran Again." She never left. She kept working in the community.
6. "Is she only a mental-health candidate?" A lane called "Beyond Mental Health." Mental health connects to housing, safety, schools, jobs, family stability, and city services.
7. "Her campaign may not look organized or polished." We do not argue it. We show disciplined, consistent content, professional presentation, schedules, neighborhood activity, and real proof.

## The clipping and seeding machine
1. Collect. Every podcast show, interview, street conversation, and other content asset goes into one shared Google Drive the day it is recorded.
2. Review. Download each recording and run it through Opus Clip. AI review finds the clips with viral potential.
3. Reposition. Each winning moment is cut with multiple hooks: direct, accountability, community, independent identity. A 40-minute interview becomes 15 to 40 assets.
4. Campaign content folder. Finished clips are stored with captions and hashtags, ready for every volunteer to post to Instagram or TikTok.
5. Post and seed. Daily Posters are recruited volunteers who post one clip a day from the folder. Seeders are owners of local and targeted theme pages, repost pages, and meme pages who push clips into audiences the campaign does not own.
6. Measure and scale. Track impressions, mentions, word of mouth, saves, reposts, and add-to-story on every clip. The winners become Facebook and Instagram ads.

## The destination
Every clip, seeder post, and ad hands off to the campaign website and app: a website, a mobile experience, and a progressive web app. The flow is capture the poll, capture the contact, then donate.

## Closing line
Promises are easy. Follow-through is the proof. D.C., we just have to win.
