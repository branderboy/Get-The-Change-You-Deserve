# Audio Conversation Overview — what the briefing should cover, in order

Six beats, about two minutes total. The hosts read the numbers as written in the pitch brief and keep the lane names exact.

## 1. The problem with promises (about 15 seconds)
Open cold: "D.C. has heard a lot of promises." This campaign is built on proof, not promises. One line on the objective: make Rhonda Hamilton known, recognizable, and credible before residents ever see an ad.

## 2. What D.C. told us (about 30 seconds)
The turn from guessing to listening. Three concerns clear seventy percent: cost of living 77, public safety 73, housing 71. Two numbers define the audience: 32 percent primary turnout, and 91,000 independents who could not vote in a closed primary. Takeaway: the audience is already defined and already asking questions.

## 3. The critics, answered (about 35 seconds)
Seven public critiques, each with a one-line answer and the name of the content lane that carries it, in this order:
1. Who is Rhonda Hamilton? "Who Is Rhonda Hamilton?" series.
2. Is she a serious citywide candidate? Consistent activity, real conversations, organization, every week.
3. What does independent mean? "Independent, Explained." Accountability to residents, not isolation.
4. What has she actually done? "Proof, Not Slogans."
5. She lost in 2022, why run again? "Why I Ran Again." She never left.
6. Is she only a mental-health candidate? "Beyond Mental Health."
7. Her campaign may not look polished. Don't argue it. Show discipline.

## 4. The machine (about 20 seconds)
Six steps, named plainly: collect, review with Opus Clip, reposition with multiple hooks, the campaign content folder, post and seed through Daily Posters and Seeders, measure and scale. End on "the winners become ads."

## 5. The destination (about 10 seconds)
Everything hands off to the campaign website and app. Capture the poll, capture the contact, then donate.

## 6. The close (about 5 seconds)
Exactly: "Promises are easy. Follow-through is the proof. D.C., we just have to win." Stop there.

## Do not
No welcome or sign-off. No opinions about the candidate or opponents. No numbers beyond the pitch brief. No summary at the end.
