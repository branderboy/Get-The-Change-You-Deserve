# Rhonda Hamilton 2026 — Asset Implementation Plan

`index.html` is the plan. This document is how it gets built. Every asset the plan commits to is listed below with the inputs it needs, the steps to produce it, what "done" means, what it depends on, and a confidence score. Nothing here goes beyond what the page states; where a step requires something the plan does not yet have, that gap is named.

**Confidence score** = how likely this asset gets produced and lands as the plan intends, given what exists today. It is not a measure of importance.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Inputs exist. Execution only. |
| 70–89 | Clear path. One input or approval still needed. |
| 50–69 | Real dependency outside our control (candidate time, third-party data, budget, platform policy). |
| Below 50 | Blocked or the plan's assumption needs checking before work starts. |

Status key: `[ ]` not started · `[~]` in progress · `[x]` done

## Sequence

Work runs in this order because each step feeds the next. Weeks are relative to kickoff, not calendar dates.

| Window | Work | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Week 0 | Close out Phase 0 assets (video). Lock brand position (4.1). Start the campaign website (0.5) so it is live before Week 4 posting begins. | A complete proposal page to share, and a destination for every handoff. |
| Weeks 1–2 | Audit insights are in (3.1). Run the first Google Trends pull (3.2) and the Reddit language guide (3.3). | The language and search signals that sharpen the packages. |
| Week 3 | Phase Two: build 4.2 system, produce 4.3, then one package per audit issue. | Scripts and hooks for production. |
| Weeks 3–4 | Candidate interview day(s): capture raw material for 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 2.6, 2.8 in one sitting. The AI briefing video (0.3) is produced in parallel and does not wait on this. | Weeks of clips from one shoot. |
| Week 4 onward | Phase Three production and posting: 5.2 and 5.3 weekly, 5.1 as podcasts land, 5.4 on a set neighborhood schedule. 5.5 live before first post. | Measured reach and the retargeting pool. |
| When organic winners are proven | Phase Six paid, only on assets with strong watch time, shares, and profile visits. | Scale and conversions. |

## Phase 2 — Confronting The Skepticism (The Crucible)

Each critique on the page gets a content lane. Every lane shares the same production path: gather proof and raw material, write hooks, shoot on interview day, cut for 5.2, archive on 5.3. The scores differ because the inputs differ.

### 2.1 "Who Is Rhonda Hamilton?" origin-story series — Confidence 80
**Basis:** Scriptable from a sit-down interview. Needs candidate time and a fact-checked bio.
- **Inputs:** biography timeline, photos, the interview day.
- **Steps:** build a life-and-work timeline; pick 5–8 story beats; shoot each as a 30–60 second answer; release one per week.
- **Done when:** the first 5 episodes are cut and scheduled.

### 2.2 Serious-candidate proof content — Confidence 65
**Basis:** The page says show "consistent activity, resident conversations, issue fluency, community proof, events, and organization." That only works if those events and that organization are actually happening on a schedule. Content can't manufacture it.
- **Inputs:** the campaign's real event calendar and field schedule.
- **Steps:** attach a camera to every event; post recap clips within 24 hours; keep a running "this week on the trail" cadence.
- **Depends on:** campaign operations, not the content team.
- **Done when:** a weekly trail recap has posted for four consecutive weeks.

### 2.3 "Independent, Explained" pillar — Confidence 85
**Basis:** A framing argument the page already states (independence as resident accountability, not isolation). Writable now.
- **Inputs:** candidate's own words on why independent.
- **Steps:** one explainer script, one myth-vs-fact swipe post, one street-conversation prompt ("what does independent mean to you?").
- **Done when:** three formats are live.

### 2.4 "Proof, Not Slogans" pillar — Confidence 75
**Basis:** The page names the record: ANC service, nonprofit leadership, mental-health advocacy, business experience, community work. Each claim must be verified with dates and names before it airs, and that verification has not been done.
- **Inputs:** documented record for each of the five areas.
- **Steps:** build a proof sheet (claim, date, source) for each area; produce one clip per verified item; never post an unverified claim.
- **Done when:** the proof sheet is complete and the five clips are cut.

### 2.5 "Why I Ran Again" response — Confidence 80
**Basis:** One direct-to-camera answer. The page already states the message: she did not disappear after 2022 and kept working in the community.
- **Inputs:** 2022-to-now activity list to back the claim.
- **Steps:** script the answer around the activity list; shoot on interview day; cut a 45-second version and a 15-second hook.
- **Done when:** both cuts are live.

### 2.6 "Beyond Mental Health" lane — Confidence 80
**Basis:** The page defines the bridge: mental health connected to housing, safety, schools, jobs, family stability, city services. Six explainers, all scriptable.
- **Inputs:** candidate's position on each connection.
- **Steps:** one explainer per connection using the Problem → City Action → Measurable Result frame from 2.7.
- **Done when:** six explainers are cut.

### 2.7 "D.C. Issues, Plainly" content — Confidence 90
**Basis:** The framework is defined (Problem → City Action → Measurable Result) and the audit has already ranked the issues.
- **Inputs:** the top-three concerns from 3.1 (cost of living, public safety, housing), then the 311 categories and the split issues.
- **Steps:** one issue per piece; write all three framework parts before shooting; no piece airs without a measurable result named.
- **Depends on:** 3.1 (done). 3.3 sharpens the phrasing.
- **Done when:** one piece exists for every issue on the priority list.

### 2.8 Plain-language public safety content — Confidence 70
**Basis:** The page names the topics (safe streets, commutes, youth safety, business safety, accountable policing). The critique is that her position is unclear, so the position must be nailed down with the candidate before anything is written.
- **Inputs:** a written safety position from the candidate.
- **Steps:** position session; one plain-language piece per topic; test each against the "would a resident understand this" benchmark from 1.1.
- **Done when:** five pieces are live and the position doc is on file.

### 2.9 Pro-resident reframe content — Confidence 75
**Basis:** A tone rule more than a single asset. It has to be applied across every piece, which makes it easy to start and easy to drift from.
- **Inputs:** none beyond the brief.
- **Steps:** add "pro-resident, pro-results, pro-accountability" to the script checklist; cut any line that reads as anti-government without a resident outcome attached.
- **Done when:** the checklist is in use and one explicit reframe piece has aired.

### 2.10 "Humanitarian" practical-standard content — Confidence 75
**Basis:** The page converts the word into a standard for renters, seniors, parents, businesses, and families in crisis. Five audience pieces, scriptable, but each needs a concrete policy example to avoid the exact vagueness being criticized.
- **Inputs:** one concrete policy example per audience.
- **Steps:** one piece per audience, each ending with what the policy does for that person.
- **Done when:** five pieces are live.

### 2.11 Organized-campaign proof — Confidence 60
**Basis:** The page's rule is right: don't argue it, show it. Showing it means the content calendar posts on schedule every week without gaps. That is an operations commitment, and it has no margin for a missed week.
- **Inputs:** a posting cadence the team can sustain.
- **Steps:** set the weekly minimum; hold it for eight weeks; keep presentation consistent (fonts, colors, lower-thirds from the page's brand).
- **Depends on:** everything in Phase 5 shipping on time.
- **Done when:** eight consecutive weeks hit the minimum.

## Phase 3 — Audit Insights (Phase One on the page)

The audit is done. Its findings are on the page as the "What D.C. Already Told Us" section, sourced from the strategy doc's data-sources appendix. What remains are the two live signals the audit refreshes monthly.

### 3.1 Audit insights on the page — Confidence 100
**Basis:** Complete. Eight insight cards and three messaging takeaways, each labeled by source type. Done.
- **Sources used:** City Cast DC / TrueDot 2026 mayoral poll (735 adults, May 12–17 2026, ±3.7); Washington Post / Schar School 2026 mayoral poll; Washington Post / Schar School August 2025 public safety poll (604 residents); DC Board of Elections 2026 primary and 2022 general turnout; DCBOE 2026 voter registration; DC Open Data 311 and City Cast's July 2026 analysis.
- **Rule carried into all content:** every finding is labeled representative polling, election behavior, government service data, search behavior, or anecdotal online sentiment. A Reddit thread is never treated as a poll.
- **Done when:** the section is live. Done.

### 3.2 Google Trends search-interest report — Confidence 90
**Basis:** Public, free, no approvals. The term list is set (Rhonda Hamilton, Janeese Lewis George, DC mayor, independent candidate DC, DC crime, DC rent, affordable housing DC, Pepco bill, Washington Gas bill, federal layoffs, DC unemployment, DC small business, DC schools, DC youth curfew, DC statehood, DC police, cost of living DC).
- **Inputs:** none.
- **Steps:** pull D.C. metro interest over 12 months for each term; capture rising related queries; separate sustained interest from one-day spikes; rank. Repeat monthly.
- **Done when:** the first report is delivered and a monthly pull is on the calendar. Measures attention, never support.

### 3.3 D.C. Language & Conversation Guide — Confidence 85
**Basis:** Sources are public (r/washingtondc, ward discussions, housing, federal-worker, small-business, and safety threads). Time-consuming but straightforward.
- **Inputs:** none.
- **Steps:** read 30 days of threads on the top-three concerns; log repeated questions, exact phrasing, misunderstandings about the election, and what residents say would make them trust a candidate; label every entry anecdotal and check it against polling or government data before it shapes a script.
- **Done when:** the guide has a phrasing section per issue and is handed to scriptwriters. Refreshed monthly.

### 3.4 Monthly insight update — Confidence 85
**Basis:** The strategy doc commits to a monthly process for updating the findings. It is a calendar commitment more than a build.
- **Inputs:** 3.2 and 3.3 outputs, the 5.5 dashboard.
- **Steps:** one page per month: what changed in search, what changed in conversation, what content responded best, and which packages to adjust.
- **Done when:** the first monthly update ships and the second is scheduled.

## Phase 5 — The Organic Content System (Phase Three on the page)

### 5.1 Podcast clipping pipeline — Confidence 70
**Basis:** The page sets a yield of 15–40 assets per 40-minute interview, which is realistic. The dependency is getting booked: no podcast appearances are scheduled yet.
- **Inputs:** booked appearances, raw audio and video from each.
- **Steps:** build a target list of D.C.-focused podcasts; pitch; after each recording, log timestamps by position; cut clips with the four hooks (Direct, Accountability, Community, Independent Identity).
- **Done when:** the first appearance is clipped to at least 15 assets.

### 5.2 Discovery content (TikTok / Reels) — Confidence 80
**Basis:** Formats are defined (Direct-to-camera, Street conversations, Issue explainers, Polls, Swipe posts, Myth-vs-Fact). Scripts come from Phases 2 and 4.
- **Inputs:** scripts, interview-day footage, accounts set up.
- **Steps:** weekly batch: one tension per piece; rotate formats; caption every piece; post on a fixed schedule.
- **Done when:** the schedule has held for four weeks.

### 5.3 Long-form hub (Facebook) — Confidence 90
**Basis:** Page setup and a posting routine. The handoff destination is the campaign website we build in 0.5.
- **Inputs:** Facebook page, campaign website URL for positions, donations, voter info.
- **Steps:** post full-length videos and interview cuts; pin the website link; reply to comments within 24 hours.
- **Depends on:** 0.5 campaign website live.
- **Done when:** the page is live with the website link pinned and a weekly long-form post.

### 5.4 Neighborhood Conversations — Confidence 70
**Basis:** Man-on-the-street needs a crew, a schedule across wards, and on-camera consent from residents. All manageable, none free.
- **Inputs:** crew, release forms, ward schedule, the 3.3 question list.
- **Steps:** one ward per outing; ask the audit's questions; capture the candidate's answers on the spot; cut into rapid-response clips.
- **Done when:** the first three wards are shot and posted.

### 5.5 Funnel tracking setup — Confidence 80
**Basis:** The four stages and their outcomes are defined on the page. "Name searches" and "site visits" come from Google Search Console and site analytics on the campaign website, which 0.5 installs on day one.
- **Inputs:** platform analytics access, website analytics, Search Console.
- **Steps:** one dashboard with the four rows (Exposure: impressions and reach; Recognition: repeat viewers and profile visits; Association: shares, saves, name searches; Interest & Handoff: site visits, warm audiences); weekly review.
- **Depends on:** 0.5 campaign website analytics.
- **Done when:** all four rows report real numbers for one full week.

## Phase 6 — The Paid Amplifier (Optional)

All three tiers share the same score and the same caveats.

**Basis for 65:** The plan makes paid conditional on organic winners, which is the right gate. Channels are Meta and Google Search. Two things sit outside our control: budget is not defined, and political ads on both platforms require advertiser identity verification and disclaimers before anything can run, which takes time. Start verification early so it is not the bottleneck when the first organic winner is ready.

### 6.1 Top funnel: Video-view and Reach ads — Confidence 65
- **Inputs:** verified ad accounts, budget, proven organic clips.
- **Steps:** promote only clips with strong organic watch time; geo-target D.C.; broad audiences.
- **Done when:** first flight runs and reach is reported in 5.5.

### 6.2 Mid funnel: retarget viewers with explainers — Confidence 65
- **Inputs:** video-view audiences from 6.1, explainers from 2.6 and 2.7.
- **Steps:** build retargeting audiences from viewers; serve explainers; measure profile visits and site visits.
- **Done when:** the retargeting pool is live and reporting.

### 6.3 Bottom funnel: RSVP, Donate, Volunteer — Confidence 65
- **Inputs:** warm audiences from 6.2, the Donate and Volunteer pages from 0.5.
- **Steps:** direct-action ads to the warmest audience only; one action per ad.
- **Depends on:** 0.5 Donate and Volunteer pages live.
- **Done when:** actions are tracked back to ad spend.
