# Strategy Briefing Video — Script (Asset 0.3)

**Length:** about 105 seconds at a conversational pace
**Format:** 16:9, AI narration, motion graphics built from the page's own cards and stats
**Palette and type:** D.C. red, slate, navy, gold; Montserrat headings, Open Sans body
**Placement:** replaces the "Play Briefing" mock player in the Crucible section

Every number below is from the audit insights on the page. Every critique and response is from the Crucible cards. Nothing new is introduced.

---

## Scene 1 — Cold open (0:00–0:08)
**Visual:** Hero photo, gradient overlay, three red stars fade in. Headline builds: "DC! We Just Have To Win."
**Narration:**
> D.C. has heard a lot of promises. This campaign is built on something different. Proof.

## Scene 2 — The objective (0:08–0:20)
**Visual:** Text on slate: "Build broad visibility. Repeated familiarity. Cultural relevance." Then the resident quote appears line by line.
**Narration:**
> The goal is simple. Make Rhonda Hamilton known, recognizable, and credible before residents ever see an ad. So that a voter says: I keep seeing her. I know who she is. She talks about D.C. the way I do. I remember her name.

## Scene 3 — What D.C. already told us (0:20–0:40)
**Visual:** Three stat tiles animate up: 77% cost of living, 73% public safety, 71% housing. Then two more: 32% primary turnout, 91,000 independents locked out. Source lines small at the bottom.
**Narration:**
> We did not guess what people care about. We listened. Cost of living, public safety, and housing all clear seventy percent as very important. Two thirds of registered voters sat out the primary. Ninety-one thousand independents could not vote in it at all. That is the audience. They are already asking questions. We answer them.

## Scene 4 — The critics, answered (0:40–1:15)
**Visual:** Seven navy cards slide in one at a time, each with red stars. Critique on top, response below. Hold each for about five seconds.
**Narration:**
> People will ask: Who is Rhonda Hamilton? We answer with her story, in a series, not a slogan.
> Is she a serious citywide candidate? We show consistent activity, real conversations, and organization, every week.
> What does independent mean? Accountability to residents, not isolation.
> What has she actually done? ANC service, nonprofit leadership, mental-health advocacy, business experience. Proof, not slogans.
> She lost in 2022, so why run again? Because she never left. She kept working.
> Is she only a mental-health candidate? Mental health connects to housing, safety, schools, jobs, and city services. We draw the line for people.
> And the one critics lean on: her campaign may not look polished. We do not argue that. We show discipline, on schedule, in every neighborhood.

## Scene 5 — The machine (1:15–1:32)
**Visual:** The six-step process map animates left to right: Collect, Review, Reposition, Library, Post & Seed, Measure. Arrow loops back to "Winners become ads."
**Narration:**
> Every interview, every podcast, every street conversation goes into one system. AI finds the moments that travel. Each one is cut into multiple hooks. Volunteers post daily from a shared library. Local pages seed the clips into audiences we do not own. The winners become ads.

## Scene 6 — Close (1:32–1:45)
**Visual:** Brand position on slate, gold on the second line: "Promises are easy. Follow-through is the proof." Three red stars. "Rhonda Hamilton for Mayor 2026."
**Narration:**
> Promises are easy. Follow-through is the proof. D.C., we just have to win.

---

## Production notes
- Narration: one AI voice, warm and direct, no announcer read. Subtitles burned in.
- Motion: cards and stats use the same shapes as the page so the video and the page read as one system.
- Music: low, steady, no drop at the end.
- Review: one pass by the campaign before the mock player is replaced.
- Hosting: YouTube unlisted or self-hosted MP4, then swap the mock player for the embed.
